№ 01 — The work

An instrument that does not need a player.

A tree cut from sheet with a modular synthesizer in its trunk. Switched on, it plays by itself, never quite repeating.

Four years in galleries and clubs. Now it needs a permanent home.

№ 02 — What it does

Three things, all at once.

01

It plays itself

Clock, chance and slow modulation drive the voices; nothing is sequenced or looped.

02

It listens to a plant

A living plant is wired into the circuit. Light, water and touch change what it plays.

03

It draws sound in fire

A metre-long Rubens’ tube on one branch: the sound sets each flame’s height, so the fire draws the music.

№ 03 — The design

The plant decides what the synth plays.

Electrodes on a leaf turn the plant’s signals into voltage, set to a musical key. Any plant works, and each plays differently.

It stands between the two synthesizer banks and needs no laptop or phone.

№ 06 — What is acquired

The whole instrument, installed and working.

The sculpture
Cut tree form, integrated console and sculpted floor. Freestanding, approx. 2 m tall, footprint approx. 2 × 1.5 m.
The instrument
Two Eurorack banks: percussion, melodic and textural voices, granular and reverb, clock and chance generators, quantisers, mixer, balanced output.
Plant interface
Biodata module and electrodes. A second plant channel on request.
Fire
One-metre Rubens’ tube with driver and mounting. Bottled propane, user-operated.
Documentation
Printed manual, two copies: modules, signal path, every connection, default patches, troubleshooting. A modular beginner can rebuild the patch from it.
Installation
Installed on site in person, with a walkthrough for whoever looks after it.
Tuning
Two sessions included to tune tempo, density, tonality, plant and fire to your room.
